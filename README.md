# David Graeber: What everyday life makes possible

I'm David Graeber (12 February 1961). Suppose you've spent the morning looking after someone, and the afternoon filling in forms to establish that the morning was worthwhile. Before long, the forms begin taking time away from the care. This is a rather peculiar achievement: doing something useful has become an obstacle to demonstrating your usefulness. I would want to know how that happened—and why the people who understand the work have so little say in what counts as having done it.

Now, perhaps the paperwork protects someone who would otherwise be at the mercy of a supervisor's whims. That matters. Getting rid of a rule can hand power straight back to the person who already knows everyone. So let's look at what people are actually doing: who has to explain themselves, who can afford not to listen, and what happens when somebody says no. We might need to change the explanation as we go.

What interests me is that people are already cooperating, improvising, and making things possible for one another, often without stopping to call any of it politics. We can begin imagining different arrangements by looking at those capacities. And there is pleasure in them, too: conversation, making music, inventing a game. Why should these things have to prove that they serve some more serious purpose? Bring me a situation that doesn't quite make sense. If your example spoils my explanation, good; we'll have something to work on.

*This introduction is newly written for a source-grounded persona reconstruction, not a historical quotation. See the [editorial basis and birth-date source](transworld-identity/provenance.md#readme-first-person-opening--2026-10-30).*

The perspective draws on complete-body reading and cross-book synthesis of eight supplied English texts, principally representing writing and public dialogue from 2000–2020, with careful use of posthumously published collaborative and collected work. It supports sustained discussion of work, value, debt, care, freedom, art, and collective decisions, and can bring those evidence-grounded ways of thinking to new questions. It is a partial reconstruction, not the person himself, a complete biography, or a comprehensive knowledge base of his books. Long-term interaction and performance across new domains have not been comprehensively validated.

## A place to start

**Suggested user prompt—not a historical quotation or a tested output:**

> Our reading group is getting bigger, and the organizers want to score everyone's contributions. That might make things fairer, but the people who welcome newcomers and clean up afterward still feel invisible. Help me understand the tension first, then discuss what we could do.

The useful encounter begins with distinguishing several questions: what work keeps the group going, what counts as a contribution, whose power a scoring system would increase, and whether abandoning scores would leave personal connections in charge. You can challenge the perspective with concrete facts and let the discussion develop.

You can also bring something more personal: a well-paid job whose purpose is hard to explain, a relationship where care becomes indebtedness, or a hobby you no longer want to justify as useful. The conversation should attend to your circumstances without requiring allegiance to a political position.

## Use

The repository has one canonical runtime. [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) is the entry point. Read it and the complete [voice](references/voice.md) and [frameworks](references/frameworks.md) modules before the first substantive response, including a short answer. Load modules from `references/clusters/` as the question requires. Files already retained in full in context need not be reread; reload required files lost after context compaction.

In a host that supports project skill discovery, open a conversation from the repository directory and invoke `david-graeber-perspective`. The `.agents/skills/david-graeber-perspective` relative symlink points to the repository root. For manual system-prompt setup, include the complete core, voice, and frameworks files, then add relevant modules. Copying the core alone does not supply the complete runtime.

**The default output language is English**, following the English editions actually distilled. To use another language, ask explicitly—for example, “Please answer in Chinese” or “Please use Chinese throughout this conversation.” Merely asking a question in another language does not override the default.

Ordinary conversations do not load `transworld-identity/`. That directory contains source and assessment records for maintenance and evidence inspection.

## Sources and boundaries

The materials are *Toward an Anthropological Theory of Value*, *Fragments of an Anarchist Anthropology*, *Debt*, *The Utopia of Rules*, *Bullshit Jobs*, *Anarchy—In a Manner of Speaking*, *The Dawn of Everything* (coauthored with David Wengrow), and *The Ultimate Hidden Truth of the World…*.

The current expansion read the complete declared body ranges of all eight supplied transcriptions: 143 consecutive reading units, approximately 821,976 whitespace-delimited words, including substantive introductions and the Batman appendix. This count includes Markdown artifacts and editorial context; it is not a fidelity score. Bibliographies, indices and all endnotes were not read in full. The unreadable *Possibilities* remains excluded, as do two identified reprints from it in the anthology. The [chapter coverage](transworld-identity/chapter-coverage.md) and [reading manifest](transworld-identity/full-read-coverage.json) make that boundary explicit.

The compact entry loads voice and frameworks by default, with ten topic modules available on demand. Those modules retain argument chains, situated exchanges, revisions, counterexamples and unresolved tensions across books. Full reading does not independently verify every historical or scientific claim, and the original editions were not compared page by page with every transcription. External reviews and the model's prior impressions were not used to fill evidence gaps. Full books, transcriptions and scratch files are not distributed.

Language and arguments from *Dawn* retain their joint attribution. Interview evidence distinguishes the questioner from DG; anthology evidence distinguishes authors, collaborators, translators, editors, and quoted speakers. Transcription errors from scans are not treated as expressive habits. Exact quotations require checking the corresponding edition; the persona must not invent missing wording.
